Lesson 30

The 5-Number Summary

Bellringer

These are the number of minutes seven students spent on a math practice set: 12, 8, 15, 9, 11, 8, 24.

  1. How could you summarize the center and spread of these times? Which value, if any, seems unusual?
    • Sort first: 8, 8, 9, 11, 12, 15, 24. The median is 11 minutes and the range is 24 − 8 = 16 minutes. The value 24 is noticeably higher and is worth checking with quartiles and outlier fences.

Big Idea: The five-number summary describes a data set’s center, spread, and range, while the IQR and outlier fences help identify unusually low or high values.

Objectives

  • Calculate the five-number summary: minimum, Q1, median (Q2), Q3, and maximum.
  • Find and interpret the interquartile range (IQR).
  • Determine lower and upper outlier fences.
  • Identify possible outliers and explain their effect on summaries.

Explore / Notes

Sort the data from least to greatest before calculating. The five-number summaryA compact description of a data set: minimum, first quartile, median, third quartile, and maximum. is:

Minimum, Q1, median (Q2), Q3, maximum

  • Minimum: the smallest actual data value.
  • Q1: the median of the lower half.
  • Median (Q2): the middle value; about half the data are at or below it.
  • Q3: the median of the upper half.
  • Maximum: the largest actual data value.
Find the Median and Quartiles
  • With an odd number of values, the median is the single middle value at position n + 12.
  • With an even number of values, average the two middle values at positions n2 and n2 + 1.
  • Find Q1 as the median of the lower half and Q3 as the median of the upper half. If n is odd, exclude the overall median from both halves.

Quartile conventions can vary by calculator or software. In this lesson, consistently use the lower-half/upper-half method above.

IQR and Outlier Fences

IQR = Q3 − Q1

Lower outlier fence = Q1 − 1.5(IQR)

Upper outlier fence = Q3 + 1.5(IQR)

Values below the lower fence or above the upper fence are potential outliers. A value exactly equal to a fence is not an outlier under this rule.

The minimum and maximum in the five-number summary are the actual data extremes.

  1. If Q1 = 18 and Q3 = 26, find the IQR and fences. Is a value equal to the lower fence an outlier?
    • IQR = 26 − 18 = 8. Lower fence = 18 − 1.5(8) = 6; upper fence = 26 + 1.5(8) = 38. A value equal to 6 is not an outlier; it must be below 6 or above 38.

Worked Examples

Example 1: Odd Number of Observations

Quiz scores: 8, 12, 5, 30, 7, 10, 4, 9, 6.

Sorted data: 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 12, 30

  1. Median: n = 9, so the median position is 9 + 12 = 5. Median (Q2) = the 5th value = 8.
  2. Halves: exclude the overall median. Lower half: 4, 5, 6, 7. Upper half: 9, 10, 12, 30.
  3. Quartiles: Q1 = 5 + 62 = 5.5. Q3 = 10 + 122 = 11.
  4. Five-number summary: (4, 5.5, 8, 11, 30).
  5. IQR: 11 − 5.5 = 5.5.
  6. Fences: lower = 5.5 − 1.5(5.5) = −2.75; upper = 11 + 1.5(5.5) = 19.25.
  7. Outliers: 30 is above 19.25, so it is a potential outlier. No value is below −2.75.
Example 2: Even Number of Observations

Hours of sleep: 5, 8, 6, 10, 7, 6, 9, 7.

Sorted data: 5, 6, 6, 7, 7, 8, 9, 10

  1. Median: n = 8, so use positions 82 = 4 and 5. Median (Q2) = 7 + 72 = 7.
  2. Halves: lower half: 5, 6, 6, 7. Upper half: 7, 8, 9, 10.
  3. Quartiles: Q1 = 6 + 62 = 6. Q3 = 8 + 92 = 8.5.
  4. Five-number summary: (5, 6, 7, 8.5, 10).
  5. IQR: 8.5 − 6 = 2.5.
  6. Fences: lower = 6 − 1.5(2.5) = 2.25; upper = 8.5 + 1.5(2.5) = 12.25.
  7. Outliers: none; every value is between 2.25 and 12.25.

Practice / Investigation

Sort each set first. Use the lower-half/upper-half convention from the notes. Show your work and explain any outlier decisions.

  1. Test scores: 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 12, 13. Find the five-number summary, IQR, fences, and any potential outliers.
    • Median = 9; lower half 5, 7, 8 gives Q1 = 7; upper half 10, 12, 13 gives Q3 = 12. Five-number summary: (5, 7, 9, 12, 13). IQR = 5; fences are −0.5 and 19.5. There are no potential outliers.
  2. Screen time (hours): 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 10, 11, 20. Find the five-number summary, IQR, fences, and any potential outlier.
    • Median = (7 + 8) / 2 = 7.5. Q1 = (4 + 5) / 2 = 4.5; Q3 = (10 + 11) / 2 = 10.5. Five-number summary: (2, 4.5, 7.5, 10.5, 20). IQR = 6; fences are −4.5 and 19.5. Since 20 > 19.5, 20 is a potential outlier.
  3. Basketball points: 8, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17. Find Q1, median, Q3, IQR, and fences.
    • Median = 13. Excluding it, lower half 8, 10, 11, 12 gives Q1 = 10.5; upper half 14, 15, 16, 17 gives Q3 = 15.5. IQR = 5. Fences are 3 and 23.
  4. Daily reading (minutes): 15, 18, 20, 22, 25, 28. Find the five-number summary and determine whether any values are potential outliers.
    • Median = (20 + 22) / 2 = 21. Q1 = (15 + 18) / 2 = 16.5; Q3 = (25 + 28) / 2 = 26.5. Five-number summary: (15, 16.5, 21, 26.5, 28). IQR = 10; fences are 1.5 and 41.5, so there are no potential outliers.
  5. Interpret: A set has Q1 = 18 and Q3 = 26. Find the IQR and fences. Are 6 and 39 potential outliers?
    • IQR = 26 − 18 = 8. Lower fence = 18 − 12 = 6; upper fence = 26 + 12 = 38. A value equal to a fence is not an outlier, so 6 is not; 39 is above the upper fence and is a potential outlier.
  6. Range vs. IQR: Compare 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 with 4, 5, 6, 7, 80. Explain how the high value affects each measure of spread.
    • The first range is 8 − 4 = 4; the second is 80 − 4 = 76, so the high value greatly increases the range. For the first set Q1 = 4.5 and Q3 = 7.5, so IQR = 3. For the second set, Q1 = 4.5 and Q3 = 7.5 as well, so IQR remains 3. The IQR is resistant because it describes the middle half rather than the extremes.

Closure

Try this new data set of minutes spent reading last night: 10, 12, 13, 14, 15, 18, 20.

  1. Find the five-number summary and both outlier fences. What do they reveal about the center, spread, and possible unusual values?
    • The data are sorted. Median = 14; Q1 = 12; Q3 = 18. Five-number summary: (10, 12, 14, 18, 20). IQR = 6; fences are 3 and 27, so there are no potential outliers. The center is 14 minutes, the middle half runs from 12 to 18 minutes, and the full range is 10 to 20 minutes.

The five-number summary shows the data extremes and the spread of the middle half; fences help flag potential outliers without replacing the actual minimum or maximum.

Lesson 31

Addition and Multiplication Rules of Probability

Bellringer

A standard deck has 52 cards, including 4 kings.

  1. What is the probability of drawing a king? If you replace the first card before drawing again, what is the probability of drawing two kings in a row?
    • P(king) = 452 = 113. With replacement, the draws are independent, so P(king and then king) = 113 × 113 = 1169.

Big Idea: Probability rules help calculate the chance that one event or multiple events occur.

Objectives

  • Calculate probabilities using the addition rule and complements.
  • Use the multiplication rule for independent events with replacement.
  • Distinguish “or” questions from “and,” “both,” and “followed by” questions.
  • Show probability calculations as simplified fractions, decimals, or percentages.

Explore / Notes

For equally likely outcomes, start with the basic probability rule:

P(A) = favorable outcomestotal outcomes

“Or” Means Add

Use an addition rule when a question asks for event A or event B.

  • Mutually exclusive eventsEvents that cannot happen at the same time in one trial. Their overlap is zero.: add the probabilities: P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B).
  • When events can overlap, subtract the shared outcomes once: P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B). They were counted in both P(A) and P(B), so subtracting the overlap corrects the double count.
“Not” Means Complement

The complementThe event that A does not happen; it includes every outcome outside event A. rule is P(not A) = 1 − P(A). For example, if everyone in a group is either married or unmarried, then P(unmarried) = 1 − P(married).

“And,” “Both,” and “Followed By” Mean Multiply for Independent Events

For independent events, one outcome does not change the probability of the next. Use P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B). With replacement, the first draw is put back before the next draw, so the probabilities stay the same and the draws are independent.

Quick cue: “or” usually asks you to add; “and,” “both,” or “followed by” usually asks you to multiply when the events are independent. Check whether events overlap and whether the draws are independent before choosing a rule.

  1. A card is drawn once. Can “draw a king” and “draw a queen” happen together on that draw? Which addition rule applies?
    • No. One card cannot be both a king and a queen, so the events are mutually exclusive and P(king or queen) = P(king) + P(queen).

Guided Examples

1. Mutually Exclusive: Brown or Hazel Eyes

In a group of 20 students, 8 have brown eyes and 5 have hazel eyes. In this example, no student is counted in both categories.

P(brown or hazel) = 820 + 520 = 1320 = 0.65 = 65%.

We add because a student in this example is in one category or the other, not both.

2. Overlapping Groups: As in Math or Science

Of 30 students, 12 earned an A in math, 10 earned an A in science, and 4 earned an A in both.

P(math A or science A) = P(math A) + P(science A) − P(both).

= 1230 + 1030 − 430 = 1830 = 35 = 0.60 = 60%.

Subtract the 4 students in both groups once because they were counted in each group.

3. Independent Draws with Replacement: Two Gray Bottles

A bag contains 5 gray and 15 other-colored bottles. Draw one, replace it, mix, and draw again.

P(gray and then gray) = P(gray) × P(gray) = 520 × 520 = 25400 = 116 = 0.0625 = 6.25%.

Replacement keeps the probability of gray at 5/20 on both draws.

4. Complement: Selecting an Unmarried Person

Suppose 18 of 30 people in a group are married. Everyone is classified as married or unmarried.

P(unmarried) = 1 − P(married) = 1 − 1830 = 1230 = 25 = 0.40 = 40%.

Give exact answers as simplified fractions when possible. A decimal or percentage can also be useful for interpretation; include enough precision and label percentages clearly.

Practice / Worksheet Connection

For each question, identify whether it asks for “or,” “not,” or independent events happening “and”/in sequence. Show the formula, substitution, and simplified answer.

  1. Eye colors (mutually exclusive or): In a group of 25 students, 10 have brown eyes and 6 have green eyes. What is the probability a randomly selected student has brown or green eyes?
    • Use addition because the categories are mutually exclusive: P(brown or green) = 1025 + 625 = 1625 = 0.64 = 64%.
  2. Cards (mutually exclusive or): Draw one card from a standard deck. What is the probability of drawing an ace or a king?
    • A card cannot be both ranks, so P(ace or king) = 452 + 452 = 852 = 213.
  3. Sports (overlap): In a group of 30 students, 14 play soccer, 11 play basketball, and 5 play both. What is the probability a student plays soccer or basketball?
    • P(soccer or basketball) = 1430 + 1130 − 530 = 2030 = 23.
  4. Punch or lemonade (overlap): At a school event, 18 students choose fruit punch, 13 choose lemonade, and 6 say they like both. Among 30 students, what fraction like punch or lemonade?
    • P(punch or lemonade) = 1830 + 1330 − 630 = 2530 = 56.
  5. Computer ownership (overlap): Of 40 students, 24 own a laptop, 17 own a desktop, and 12 own both. What is the probability a student owns a laptop or desktop?
    • P(laptop or desktop) = 2440 + 1740 − 1240 = 2940.
  6. Same color with replacement: A bag has 5 red and 15 blue tokens. Draw a token, replace it, and draw again. What is the probability both draws are red?
    • Replacement makes the draws independent: P(red and red) = 520 × 520 = 25400 = 116.
  7. Ordered different colors with replacement: A bag has 3 red, 4 green, and 5 blue tokens. Draw with replacement. What is the probability of red first, followed by green?
    • Order matters, and replacement keeps the draws independent: P(red then green) = 312 × 412 = 12144 = 112.
  8. Complement: In a group of 35 adults, 21 are married. What is the probability a randomly selected adult is unmarried?
    • P(unmarried) = 1 − P(married) = 1 − 2135 = 1435 = 25.

Closure

  1. Overlap “or”: Of 20 students, 9 play soccer, 8 play basketball, and 3 play both. Identify the rule and find P(soccer or basketball).
    • This is the addition rule for overlapping events: P(soccer or basketball) = 920 + 820 − 320 = 1420 = 710.
  2. Two draws with replacement: A bag has 2 yellow and 6 purple tiles. A tile is drawn, replaced, and drawn again. Identify the rule and find P(yellow both times).
    • These are independent events with replacement, so use multiplication: P(yellow and yellow) = 28 × 28 = 464 = 116.

Identify the event relationship first: “or” uses addition, “not” uses a complement, and independent “and” events use multiplication.

Introduction to StatCrunch

Getting Started

Big Idea: StatCrunch uses technology to calculate statistics and perform inference, but students must still choose the correct procedure and interpret the results.

Open StatCrunch

  1. Open StatCrunch using the link above.
  2. Follow the on-screen account creation or access prompts, then sign in. Access may depend on your Pearson account or your school's subscription; availability and sign-in options can vary.
  3. When the workspace opens, use the blank data table. Enter numerical observations down one column, one value per row. Use the column heading (such as var1) to label what the data represent.
  4. Use the Data menu to open, load, or import a data set when data are already saved elsewhere. Follow the prompts and check that columns and labels imported as expected.
  5. Use Stat to find statistical procedures. After choosing the procedure and entering the inputs, click Compute! to run it.

For official walkthroughs and help topics, use StatCrunch Help & Training.

  • Construct and interpret a t confidence interval for a population mean when σ is unknown.
  • Find a confidence interval for population standard deviation σ using chi-square methods.
  • Run and interpret a right-tailed one-proportion hypothesis test.
  • Record the menu path, inputs, output, and a contextual interpretation for every procedure.

Descriptive Statistics: Mean, Median, 5-Number Summary & Standard Deviation

Use this small data set of hours of sleep: 6, 7, 8, 8, 9, 10, 11, 13.

  1. Enter or open the numerical data in one column.
  2. Select Stat → Summary Stats → Columns.
  3. Select the sleep-hours column. The official StatCrunch help walkthrough proceeds directly to Compute!; some versions or dialogs may have an intermediate selection screen.
  4. StatCrunch's default column summary includes mean, median, standard deviation, minimum, Q1, Q3, maximum, and range. If your dialog allows selecting statistics, choose the measures you need.
  5. Click Compute! and read the output table. Match each statistic label to its meaning and units.

Check the output: The mean is 9 hours; the median is 8.5 hours. The five-number summary is minimum 6, Q1 7.5, median 8.5, Q3 10.5, maximum 13 hours (using the median-of-halves convention).

StatCrunch reports a sample standard deviation, s ≈ 2.27 hours. The population standard deviation, σ, describes the whole population and is usually unknown. Do not confuse the sample statistic s with the population parameter σ.

StatCrunch calculates the median and quartiles automatically. Quartile values can differ slightly from a hand calculation because calculators and software may use different quartile conventions; use the method requested in your course when comparing answers.

Official help: Summary statistics for columns

Confidence Interval for a Population Proportion

A school poll finds that 58 of 100 randomly selected students support a later start time. We want a 95% confidence interval for the population proportion p.

  1. Select Stat → Proportion Stats → One Sample → With Summary.
  2. Enter # of successes = 58 and # of observations = 100.
  3. Under Perform, choose Confidence interval for p.
  4. Enter Level = 0.95 for 95% confidence.
  5. Click Compute!.
  6. Read the sample proportion and the output's L. Limit and U. Limit; state what the interval estimates in context.

Here p̂ = 58100 = 0.58. Using the default Standard-Wald method, the standard error is √0.58(0.42)100 ≈ 0.0494. A 95% interval is approximately 0.58 ± 1.96(0.0494) = (0.483, 0.677).

Interpretation: We are 95% confident that the population proportion of students who support a later start is between about 48.3% and 67.7%. The confidence level describes the long-run success rate of the interval method over repeated random samples, not the chance that this particular fixed p moves around.

Official help: Proportion with summary data

Confidence Interval for a Population Mean Using t

Use a one-sample t interval when σ is unknown and you use sample standard deviation s.

  1. Select Stat → T Stats → One Sample.
  2. Choose With Data for raw observations or With Summary if you have only x̄, s, and n.
  3. For raw data, select the data column. For summary data, enter the sample mean, sample standard deviation, and sample size.
  4. Under Perform, select Confidence interval for μ.
  5. Enter the confidence level (for example, 0.95) and click Compute!.
  6. Read the lower and upper limits and the degrees of freedom, df = n − 1. The t-distribution is appropriate because σ is unknown and s estimates it.

Worked example: For the sleep data above, x̄ = 9, s ≈ 2.27, n = 8, and df = 7. A 95% t-critical value is about 2.365. The standard error is 2.27√8 ≈ 0.802, so the interval is 9 ± 2.365(0.802) ≈ (7.10, 10.90) hours.

Interpretation: We are 95% confident that the population mean sleep time represented by this sample is between about 7.10 and 10.90 hours.

Official help: Mean with summary data · Mean with raw data

Confidence Interval for Population Standard Deviation Using Chi-Square

A chi-square interval for population standard deviation σ assumes a random sample from a normally distributed population. StatCrunch's public Help & Training index currently does not document a one-sample variance/standard-deviation confidence-interval procedure, and the full procedure dialog may depend on your signed-in access/version.

  1. If your Stat menu offers Variance Stats → One Sample, open it and choose With Data or With Summary according to the information you have.
  2. Select or enter the data or summary values requested by the dialog.
  3. If the dialog offers a confidence interval for population variance or standard deviation, select the interval procedure and enter the confidence level. Click Compute!.
  4. Read the limits and check whether they are for variance σ² or standard deviation σ. If the output is a variance interval (L, U), take square roots of both endpoints: (√L, √U) is the corresponding interval for σ.
  5. If your signed-in interface does not provide this interval procedure, use StatCrunch Summary Stats to obtain s, then use the chi-square critical values from your course table and the formula below. Do not substitute z or t critical values.

For a two-sided confidence interval with df = n − 1, use the chi-square critical values for the assigned tail areas:

√(n − 1)s²χ²R < σ < √(n − 1)s²χ²L

The right-tail value χ²R gives the lower endpoint; the left-tail value χ²L gives the upper endpoint.

Example: For n = 10 and s = 4, df = 9. At 95% confidence, χ²R ≈ 19.023 and χ²L ≈ 2.700. Lower = √(9)(4²)19.023 ≈ 2.75; upper = √(9)(4²)2.700 ≈ 7.30. The interval for σ is approximately (2.75, 7.30) measurement units.

See StatCrunch Help & Training for current procedures available in your account.

One-Proportion Hypothesis Test: Greater-Than Alternative

A company claims that 50% of customers prefer a product. In a random sample of 100 customers, 58 prefer it. Test whether the true proportion is greater than 50% at α = 0.05.

H₀: p = p₀ = 0.50

Hₐ: p > p₀ = 0.50

  1. Select Stat → Proportion Stats → One Sample → With Summary.
  2. Enter # of successes = 58 and # of observations = 100.
  3. Under Perform, select Hypothesis test for p.
  4. Enter null proportion 0.50.
  5. Set the alternative to > (greater than).
  6. Click Compute!.
  7. Find Z-Stat and P-value. Here p̂ = 0.58 and z = 0.58 − 0.50√(0.50)(0.50)100 = 1.60; the right-tail P-value is about 0.0548.
  8. Compare 0.0548 with α = 0.05. Since P-value > α, fail to reject H₀. State the conclusion in context.

Conclusion: At the 5% significance level, the sample does not provide sufficient evidence that more than 50% of customers prefer the product. Failing to reject H₀ does not prove that H₀ is true.

Decision rule: If P-value ≤ α, reject H₀; otherwise, fail to reject H₀.

Official help: One-sample proportion test with summary data
