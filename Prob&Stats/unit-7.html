Lesson 30

The 5-Number Summary

Bellringer

These are the number of minutes seven students spent on a math practice set: 12, 8, 15, 9, 11, 8, 24.

  1. How could you summarize the center and spread of these times? Which value, if any, seems unusual?
    • Sort first: 8, 8, 9, 11, 12, 15, 24. The median is 11 minutes and the range is 24 − 8 = 16 minutes. The value 24 is noticeably higher and is worth checking with quartiles and outlier fences.

Big Idea: The five-number summary describes a data set’s center, spread, and range, while the IQR and outlier fences help identify unusually low or high values.

Objectives

  • Calculate the five-number summary: minimum, Q1, median (Q2), Q3, and maximum.
  • Find and interpret the interquartile range (IQR).
  • Determine lower and upper outlier fences.
  • Identify possible outliers and explain their effect on summaries.

Explore / Notes

Sort the data from least to greatest before calculating. The five-number summaryA compact description of a data set: minimum, first quartile, median, third quartile, and maximum. is:

Minimum, Q1, median (Q2), Q3, maximum

  • Minimum: the smallest actual data value.
  • Q1: the median of the lower half.
  • Median (Q2): the middle value; about half the data are at or below it.
  • Q3: the median of the upper half.
  • Maximum: the largest actual data value.
Find the Median and Quartiles
  • With an odd number of values, the median is the single middle value at position n + 12.
  • With an even number of values, average the two middle values at positions n2 and n2 + 1.
  • Find Q1 as the median of the lower half and Q3 as the median of the upper half. If n is odd, exclude the overall median from both halves.

Quartile conventions can vary by calculator or software. In this lesson, consistently use the lower-half/upper-half method above.

IQR and Outlier Fences

IQR = Q3 − Q1

Lower outlier fence = Q1 − 1.5(IQR)

Upper outlier fence = Q3 + 1.5(IQR)

Values below the lower fence or above the upper fence are potential outliers. A value exactly equal to a fence is not an outlier under this rule.

The minimum and maximum in the five-number summary are the actual data extremes.

  1. If Q1 = 18 and Q3 = 26, find the IQR and fences. Is a value equal to the lower fence an outlier?
    • IQR = 26 − 18 = 8. Lower fence = 18 − 1.5(8) = 6; upper fence = 26 + 1.5(8) = 38. A value equal to 6 is not an outlier; it must be below 6 or above 38.

Worked Examples

Example 1: Odd Number of Observations

Quiz scores: 8, 12, 5, 30, 7, 10, 4, 9, 6.

Sorted data: 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 12, 30

  1. Median: n = 9, so the median position is 9 + 12 = 5. Median (Q2) = the 5th value = 8.
  2. Halves: exclude the overall median. Lower half: 4, 5, 6, 7. Upper half: 9, 10, 12, 30.
  3. Quartiles: Q1 = 5 + 62 = 5.5. Q3 = 10 + 122 = 11.
  4. Five-number summary: (4, 5.5, 8, 11, 30).
  5. IQR: 11 − 5.5 = 5.5.
  6. Fences: lower = 5.5 − 1.5(5.5) = −2.75; upper = 11 + 1.5(5.5) = 19.25.
  7. Outliers: 30 is above 19.25, so it is a potential outlier. No value is below −2.75.
Example 2: Even Number of Observations

Hours of sleep: 5, 8, 6, 10, 7, 6, 9, 7.

Sorted data: 5, 6, 6, 7, 7, 8, 9, 10

  1. Median: n = 8, so use positions 82 = 4 and 5. Median (Q2) = 7 + 72 = 7.
  2. Halves: lower half: 5, 6, 6, 7. Upper half: 7, 8, 9, 10.
  3. Quartiles: Q1 = 6 + 62 = 6. Q3 = 8 + 92 = 8.5.
  4. Five-number summary: (5, 6, 7, 8.5, 10).
  5. IQR: 8.5 − 6 = 2.5.
  6. Fences: lower = 6 − 1.5(2.5) = 2.25; upper = 8.5 + 1.5(2.5) = 12.25.
  7. Outliers: none; every value is between 2.25 and 12.25.

Practice / Investigation

Sort each set first. Use the lower-half/upper-half convention from the notes. Show your work and explain any outlier decisions.

  1. Test scores: 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 12, 13. Find the five-number summary, IQR, fences, and any potential outliers.
    • Median = 9; lower half 5, 7, 8 gives Q1 = 7; upper half 10, 12, 13 gives Q3 = 12. Five-number summary: (5, 7, 9, 12, 13). IQR = 5; fences are −0.5 and 19.5. There are no potential outliers.
  2. Screen time (hours): 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 10, 11, 20. Find the five-number summary, IQR, fences, and any potential outlier.
    • Median = (7 + 8) / 2 = 7.5. Q1 = (4 + 5) / 2 = 4.5; Q3 = (10 + 11) / 2 = 10.5. Five-number summary: (2, 4.5, 7.5, 10.5, 20). IQR = 6; fences are −4.5 and 19.5. Since 20 > 19.5, 20 is a potential outlier.
  3. Basketball points: 8, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17. Find Q1, median, Q3, IQR, and fences.
    • Median = 13. Excluding it, lower half 8, 10, 11, 12 gives Q1 = 10.5; upper half 14, 15, 16, 17 gives Q3 = 15.5. IQR = 5. Fences are 3 and 23.
  4. Daily reading (minutes): 15, 18, 20, 22, 25, 28. Find the five-number summary and determine whether any values are potential outliers.
    • Median = (20 + 22) / 2 = 21. Q1 = (15 + 18) / 2 = 16.5; Q3 = (25 + 28) / 2 = 26.5. Five-number summary: (15, 16.5, 21, 26.5, 28). IQR = 10; fences are 1.5 and 41.5, so there are no potential outliers.
  5. Interpret: A set has Q1 = 18 and Q3 = 26. Find the IQR and fences. Are 6 and 39 potential outliers?
    • IQR = 26 − 18 = 8. Lower fence = 18 − 12 = 6; upper fence = 26 + 12 = 38. A value equal to a fence is not an outlier, so 6 is not; 39 is above the upper fence and is a potential outlier.
  6. Range vs. IQR: Compare 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 with 4, 5, 6, 7, 80. Explain how the high value affects each measure of spread.
    • The first range is 8 − 4 = 4; the second is 80 − 4 = 76, so the high value greatly increases the range. For the first set Q1 = 4.5 and Q3 = 7.5, so IQR = 3. For the second set, Q1 = 4.5 and Q3 = 7.5 as well, so IQR remains 3. The IQR is resistant because it describes the middle half rather than the extremes.

Closure

Try this new data set of minutes spent reading last night: 10, 12, 13, 14, 15, 18, 20.

  1. Find the five-number summary and both outlier fences. What do they reveal about the center, spread, and possible unusual values?
    • The data are sorted. Median = 14; Q1 = 12; Q3 = 18. Five-number summary: (10, 12, 14, 18, 20). IQR = 6; fences are 3 and 27, so there are no potential outliers. The center is 14 minutes, the middle half runs from 12 to 18 minutes, and the full range is 10 to 20 minutes.

The five-number summary shows the data extremes and the spread of the middle half; fences help flag potential outliers without replacing the actual minimum or maximum.
